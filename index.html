Know the organization

The Fireweed Project, from pilot to hotline

Fireweed is the plant that grows back first after a forest fire. A pilot participant shared that it was traditionally used as birth control and to end a pregnancy, and the name honours that.

  1. 2020
    Call Auntie launches as a COVID-19 Indigenous Pathways Hotline run by Indigenous midwives and community health workers. Renée publishes a commentary on COVID, abortion access, and bridging Western and Indigenous knowledges.
  2. 2021
    Pilot, "Global Goal, Local Impact" (UVic). Fifteen conversational interviews, Sept–Nov 2021, guided by an Indigenous Advisory Committee of four frontline providers.
  3. 2022
    Phase 1, "The Right to Abortion Project," funded by the SSHRC Race, Gender and Diversity Initiative (about $448K over 3 years). It was co-led with four community organizations: Abortion Support Services Atlantic, ekw'í7tl, the Northern Reproductive Justice Network and Northern Manitoba Abortion Support. Renée also receives a Michael Smith Health Research BC Scholar award.

What Phase 1 found

Barriers (Contraception, 2023; n=15 from Anishinaabe, Cree, Dene, Haudenosaunee, Inuit, Métis and Mi'kmaq Nations): logistical barriers, poor treatment, stigma, colonialism's effect on attitudes toward abortion, traditional knowledge, and follow-up care and support. Reproductive coercion also came up.

Recommendations (SRHM, 2023): choice of where the abortion happens; holistic post-abortion support; non-judgmental information; support persons and advocacy (booking, travel, appointments); and cultural safety grounded in local practices.

Biggest gap: post-abortion support. That finding led to the aftercare kits, the Honouring Your Journey booklet, and now the hotline.

What From Research to Action is building

  • A culturally safe abortion support hotline, co-led by Call Auntie / SGMT and ekw'í7tl, with trained navigators.
  • An Indigenous-led evaluation of the hotline. Monique Auger and Astrid Pérez Piñán were named on evaluation.
  • A cultural safety credential module for abortion providers.
  • Five years, national, funded by CIHR, now based at Unity Health / MAP.

What Fireweed offers today

  • Post-abortion support kits for First Nations, Métis and Inuit people aged 12+, shipped discreetly. They hold comfort items, traditional medicines and teachings.
  • Honouring Your Journey, a resource booklet that runs from access information to emotional and spiritual recovery.
  • The "Abortion is Traditional" zine.
  • A template letter to request taking the products of conception home, in English and French. It's a strong concrete example of cultural safety.
  • A directory of Indigenous doulas and organizations offering abortion support, including Call Auntie and ekw'í7tl.
  • For providers: the Enabling Indigenous Practices in Procedural Abortion guidebook (smudging, taking remains home, support persons), a kit poster, patient handouts and training links.

Be fluent in the landscape

Policy and reproductive justice context, 2026

You won't be quizzed on all of this. Knowing it lets you connect any answer to the bigger picture.

Forced and coerced sterilization

Bill S-228 (Senator Yvonne Boyer, Métis) received royal assent on June 15, 2026. It makes sterilization without consent an aggravated assault in the Criminal Code. Survivors' groups are shaping the implementation guidance. Background: the Senate's 2022 report The Scars that We Carry, class actions in several provinces, and Boyer and Bartlett's 2017 Saskatoon review.

Birth alerts

Every province had ended them by April 2023 (Quebec was last). Advocates say informal apprehensions continue. This is part of why trust in health systems is low.

Funding

The federal Sexual and Reproductive Health Fund was cut in 2024–25. In March 2026 about $5.4M was announced for five national organizations, including $949K to Abortion Care Canada partly for Indigenous, 2SLGBTQI+ and disabled people's access. Advocates, including Action Canada, say it falls short.

Pharmacare and contraception

Bilateral deals cover contraception in Manitoba, BC, PEI and Yukon. Ontario has none, and new deals appear stalled in 2026. This matters for a Toronto-based hotline that gives callers information.

Mifepristone

Pharmacists have been able to dispense it since 2017, but access is uneven: 34% of BC pharmacies couldn't dispense within 3 days (2025), and an Ontario study called it "approved but unavailable." NIHB covers it for many First Nations and Inuit people. Travel costs usually aren't covered.
